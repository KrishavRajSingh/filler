# What to put for "endpoint" on your NPI application (and 5 other fields that trip people up)

*Subtitle: The NPPES form is short, but a handful of its fields don't explain what they want.*

---

I build a browser tool that fills out forms, which means I read a lot of forms very slowly. The NPI application surprised me. It's not long, but several of its labels leave you guessing about what the reviewer expects, and those are the fields people end up searching for halfway through.

If you're a nurse, therapist, pharmacist or physician getting your National Provider Identifier for the first time, these are the ones to know about before you start.

## 1. Endpoint

You can leave it blank. The endpoint section is optional.

An endpoint is a secure electronic address that other providers use to send you health information, usually a Direct messaging address or a FHIR URL that your EHR vendor gives you. It isn't your regular email address, even though the label makes it sound like it could be.

If your practice doesn't use one yet, skip it and add it later by updating your NPPES record. If you do have one, you'll choose the endpoint type (Direct, FHIR, REST, SOAP or other) and the practice location it belongs to.

## 2. SSN

The official CMS-10114 instructions call the SSN optional. Leave it out, though, and you have to send two proofs of identity, and your application may take longer. If you don't qualify for an SSN you can give an ITIN instead. Don't put an EIN in either field.

If you do give an SSN, your name and date of birth have to match what Social Security has on file. Use your full legal name with no initials.

## 3. Practice location

Your primary practice location and phone number show up on the public NPI Registry. The form asks you not to use your home address unless it's also where you do business. If you work from home, sort out a business address before you apply.

## 4. Taxonomy code

You need at least one 10-character taxonomy code, and one of them has to be marked primary. People often pick a code that describes their degree when the form wants their specialty. Look yours up in the NUCC taxonomy list rather than guessing from the dropdown. Residents who don't have a license yet can use the student taxonomy code.

## 5. Type 1 or Type 2

Type 1 is for individual providers, and that includes sole proprietors. Type 2 is for organizations such as group practices, hospitals, pharmacies and labs.

An individual gets one NPI for life, however many licenses, specialties or locations they have. If you already have one, update it instead of applying again.

## 6. The 30-day rule

When you sign the certification, you agree to report changes like a new address or a new license within 30 days. I'd put a calendar reminder in the day you move, since it's easy to forget a promise you made on a signup form.

## Online or paper

Apply online through NPPES if you can. You'll need an Identity & Access (I&A) account first, and you log in to NPPES with it. The paper form, CMS-10114, still exists. You mail it to CMS NPI Enumerator Services in Baltimore, and it's slower.
